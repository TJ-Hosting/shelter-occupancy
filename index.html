      06 · About the data

      What this shows, and what it doesn't

      What it shows

      • The City of Toronto's own nightly count of people staying in shelter and overnight programs it funds, and how many of those programs' beds and rooms were occupied. The count is taken at 4:00 am and refers to the evening before.
      • Beds and rooms are separate measures and are never added together. Rates are occupied ÷ actual capacity (beds or rooms in service that night), the City's own formula, applied to the totals.
      • “Temporary Refugee Response” programs appear in their own panel. The headline covers every other program area.
      • Unmatched callers come from a second City dataset, Central Intake's service queue, published monthly with its own dates.

      What it doesn't

      • It is not a count of homelessness in Toronto. People sleeping outside, staying with others or in programs the City does not track here are not in it, and this page adds no outside estimates.
      • It cannot tell you whether a bed is free right now. The City notes that unoccupied beds and rooms “may be held for a service user or may be vacant”, and programs with nobody staying that night are left out of the day's data.
      • It holds nothing about individuals. The data is program-level.

      “At or near full”

      This page's own definition, not the City's: a program whose City-reported occupancy rate is 95% or more, meaning at most 1 in 20 of its in-service beds or rooms unoccupied. Every rule and transformation is documented.

      The City's limitation, verbatim

      This is unaudited data compiled directly from an administrative database. Data reflect only the state of each program's records in the database and may not always accurately reflect the actual current situation in each program.
