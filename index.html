What are these?

Temporary Programs. The City's field notes don't define this program area. It first appears on January 1, 2024, when the City moved its 22 “COVID-19 Response” programs into it, so this page counts COVID-19 Response (2021–2023) with it and the headline series has no break at the relabel. It is kept out of the headline so that an undefined category doesn't drive it.

Temporary Refugee Response. The City's definition: “Programs that are intended to create spaces in the overnight services system in order to support refugee and asylum claimant families and individuals.” Year-round refugee programs (“Base Program - Refugee”) stay in the headline.

Unmatched callers come from a second City dataset, Central Intake's service queue: “Total individuals who were not able to be offered a space over the past 24 hours, at time of closeout at 4am” (operational date —). Singles and couples only; the City publishes it monthly, so it lags the occupancy count.

About this page

  • What it is: the City of Toronto's own nightly count of people staying in the shelter and overnight programs it funds, and how many of their beds and rooms were occupied. It is taken at 4 am and refers to the evening before; every figure carries its date.
  • What it isn't: a count of homelessness in Toronto, or a list of free beds. People sleeping outside or staying with others aren't in it, and the City notes that unoccupied beds and rooms “may be held for a service user or may be vacant”.
  • “At or near full” is this page's own term, not the City's: a program whose City-reported occupancy rate is 95% or more.
The City's limitation, verbatim

This is unaudited data compiled directly from an administrative database. Data reflect only the state of each program's records in the database and may not always accurately reflect the actual current situation in each program.
